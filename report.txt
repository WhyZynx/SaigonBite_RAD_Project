Executive Summary (0.25 point)
SaigonBite is a centralized cloud-kitchen food delivery platform designed to bridge the operational gap between customers, cloud-kitchen hubs, shippers, administrators, and business managers. Currently, reliance on manual communication and disconnected tools across order placement, kitchen preparation, delivery coordination, and payment tracking leads to delayed updates, item unavailability conflicts, and inconsistent operational records.
To address these challenges, Team F3 (comprising Vo Ngoc Thanh Vy, Nguyen Ly Bao Tran, and Tran Thi Ngoc Nhu) conducted a comprehensive Requirements Analysis and Design (RAD) study following an iterative six-iteration methodology. In the Business Requirements phase, the team defined the system vision, identified five core stakeholder groups, established a 25-requirement backlog (22 Functional and 3 Non-Functional Requirements), and modeled end-to-end business workflows alongside a Risk and Assumption Log.
During System Requirements Analysis, 16 candidate use cases were identified and scoped down to six core use cases for detailed specification: UC01 ­ Place an Order, UC02 ­ Track an Order, UC03 ­ Confirm/Reject Incoming Order, UC04 ­ Prepare Order and Mark Ready, UC05 ­ Manage Vouchers, and UC06 ­ Produce Management Reports. These workflows were rigorously analyzed using fully developed use case descriptions, Activity Diagrams, System Sequence Diagrams (SSDs), State Machine Diagrams, a generalized Domain Class Model, and CRUD verification matrices.
In the System Requirements Design phase, the team translated the analytical models into a decoupled, multi-layered object-oriented architecture (Boundary/UI, Controller, Service, Domain/DTO, and Data Access layers). Complete First-Cut Design Class Diagrams, CRC Cards, OOD Sequence Diagrams, and Final Design Class Diagrams were produced for all six use cases, incorporating design principles such as High Cohesion, Low Coupling, and Dependency Injection via repository interfaces (IOrderRepository, IVoucherRepository, IReportRepository, etc.). Collectively, these artifacts establish a consistent, traceable technical blueprint ready for database mapping, software implementation, and system testing

I. Business Requirements (1 point)
1.1 System Vision and Approval Record (author: 524H0040)

1.1.1 Problem Description
SaigonBite is an online food delivery platform that connects customers with cloud kitchens through a centralized system. In the current business process, order coordination, kitchen updates, delivery arrangements, and payment records may rely on manual communication and separate tools. This can make it difficult to track order progress, coordinate activities between stakeholders, and maintain consistent information. In addition, problems such as unavailable food items, payment discrepancies, and customer complaints may require extra time to resolve. Therefore, a centralized platform is needed to improve order coordination, information transparency, and operational management.

1.1.2 System Capabilities
SaigonBite will provide the following capabilities:  Order Management: Allow customers to browse available menus, select food items, enter delivery information, choose a payment method, and place orders.  Kitchen Order Processing: Allow kitchen staff to review incoming orders, confirm or reject them, and update accepted orders as they are prepared and marked READY.  Order Tracking and Delivery Coordination: Provide order status information and support coordination between kitchen staff, shippers, and customers.  Payment Recording: Record payment information and support the tracking of payment status.  Voucher Management: Allow administrators to create, view, update, and archive promotional vouchers.  Complaint and Refund Recording: Support the recording and handling of customer complaints and refund requests.  Management Reporting: Provide business reports on sales, hub workload, and delivery performance.

1.1.3 Business Benefits
SaigonBite is expected to provide the following business benefits:  Improved Operational Efficiency: Reduce manual coordination and help stakeholders manage orders more efficiently.  Greater Order Transparency: Make order status information easier for customers and staff to access.  Reduced Processing Errors: Centralize order and payment records to reduce inconsistencies and recording mistakes.  Better Customer Service: Help customers follow order progress and provide a clearer process for handling complaints.  Improved Management Oversight: Help managers monitor sales and operational performance through centralized reports.

II. System Requirements Analysis (15 marks)
2.1 Translate from Business Use Case
2.1.1 System Narrative
SaigonBite is an online food delivery system that supports order placement, kitchen order processing, order tracking, delivery coordination, voucher management, and business reporting. Customers can browse available hubs and menus, select food items, enter delivery information, apply a voucher if applicable, choose a payment method, and submit an order. Before creating the order, the system validates the order information, item availability, and delivery coverage.
If the order information is invalid, the system informs the customer so that the information can be corrected and resubmitted. If the selected items are unavailable or the delivery address is outside the supported area, the system notifies the customer and prevents the order from proceeding. For valid orders, the system records the order with a PENDING status and sends it to the kitchen staff for review.
Kitchen staff can accept or reject an incoming order. If the order is rejected, the system records the rejection and notifies the customer. If it is accepted, the system updates the order status to CONFIRMED. Kitchen staff then prepare the food and mark the order as READY. The system records status changes so customers can track their orders. A shipper collects the ready order and delivers it to the customer, after which the system records the delivery outcome and updates the order status.
The system also records payment information according to the selected payment method. For online payments, payment status is recorded based on the payment result. For cash on delivery (COD), the payment remains pending until the cash collection is recorded. If a customer submits a complaint or refund request, the system supports recording the request and its resolution. Administrators can manage promotional vouchers, while managers can access reports on sales, hub workload, and delivery performance to monitor business operations.

ACTIVE: The initial state when a voucher is successfully created by the Admin. It is available for customers to apply to their orders.
EXPIRED: The system automatically transitions the voucher to this state when the current system date exceeds the voucher's end_date. It can no longer be used.

4.3 Implementation

4.3.1 Map Persistent Objects to the Tables in a Database
To transform the object-oriented Design Class Diagram into a relational database schema, classes are first categorized into transient and persistent objects. All interface, control, service, data access, and data transfer classes (<<boundary>>, <<controller>>, <<service>>, <<interface>>, <<dataAccess>>, and ReportMetrics <<DTO>>) exist only in memory during runtime and are excluded from database persistence. The remaining 9 persistent entity classes (<<entity>>) are mapped to relational tables using the following Object-to-Relational (O/R) mapping rules:
 Naming & Data Type Conversion: PascalCase class names and camelCase attributes are converted to snake_case plural table names and column names. UML data types are mapped to standard SQL types (Integer  INT, String  VARCHAR, Float  FLOAT, Boolean  BOOLEAN, DateTime  DATETIME).
 Inheritance Mapping (Single Table Strategy): The generalization hierarchy of Customer (LocalCustomer and TouristCustomer) is flattened into a single customers table using a customer_type discriminator column (LOCAL or TOURIST), allowing subtype-specific attributes (phone, email, passport_number) to be nullable.
 Association & Key Mapping: For one-to-many relationships (1 to 0..* or 1..*), the Primary Key (PK) of the parent table is propagated as a Foreign Key (FK) in the child table (orders.voucher_id remains nullable to reflect the optional 0..1 multiplicity). For the one-to-zero-or-one relationship (1 to 0..1) between Order and Delivery, order_id is placed in deliveries as a Foreign Key with a UNIQUE constraint.
